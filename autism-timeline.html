Autism: Timeline

A history of the concept (in progress)

Compiled from Silberman’s NeuroTribes (2015) and Verywell Health. A work in progress.

The Discovery Era

  • 1911 — Bleuler coins the term “autism,” referring to the withdrawn, “obsessive” behaviors of children exhibiting a form of childhood schizophrenia.
  • 1926 — Grunya Sukhareva, a child psychiatrist in Kyiv, writes about six children with autistic traits in Monatsschrift für Psychiatrie und Neurologie.
  • 1931 — Hans Asperger joins the children's clinic in Austria.
  • 1938 — Louise Despert, a psychologist in New York, details 29 cases of childhood schizophrenia, some resembling today's classification of autism.
  • 1943 — Kanner publishes his paper.
  • 1944 — Asperger publishes his paper (in German).

The Publishing Era

  • 1952 — DSM-I released.
  • 1964 — Rimland publishes; Lovaas publishes.

The DSM Era

  • 1970 — Lorna Wing popularizes the spectrum concept.
  • 1980 — DSM-III includes infantile autism as a category.
  • 1988 — the movie Rain Man is released.
  • 1990 — IDEA includes autism as a disability category.
  • 1994 — DSM-IV uses spectrum language.
  • 1996 — Temple Grandin publishes her autobiography.
