Gabriel Esquivel

Special education researcher · Debate educator · Applied AI

I'm an education researcher focused on how AI tools can work alongside evidence-based practices to reduce educational disparities for students with disabilities. My research practice grew out of eight years of competitive debate, developed through six years at a debate education startup in Taiwan, and is now grounded in formal training at the University of Kansas. I prepare English-language learners in grades 2–12 to compete against native speakers at international debate tournaments — and I build the measurement tools my classroom needs and does not yet have.

Taipei, Taiwan · email in the footer

Now — Asian Debate League, Senior Partner & Researcher (2023–present)

  • Developed an experimental gamification curriculum with student surveys and peer-feedback forms measuring anxiety, evidence usage, rule adherence, and topic understanding.
  • Teach roughly 50 students a semester; judge and evaluate roughly 120 debates a semester across four tournaments.
  • Mentor 7 junior instructors and researchers across four year-long policy topics, with quotas and assessments to track progress and quality.
  • Built an automated attendance-tracking workflow across three teachers using Claude Code, and a locally-hosted LLM reporting pipeline that ingests teacher observations into PostgreSQL and produces quarterly parent reports.
  • Recruited 30 new students in Taichung — a 10% increase in active students.

Before — Junior Instructor (2019–2023)

  • Designed and delivered debate curriculum for ESL students across grades 2–12, calibrating interventions, reinforcements, and cultural expectations to each developmental stage and to neurodiversity.
  • Collaborated with researchers, designers, Mandarin translators, and executives on curriculum design, student placement, and progress tracking.
  • Produce bi-monthly policy education briefs spanning AI governance, American economics, and international relations.

Classes I teach

Policy Debate

The deep end: a year-long topic, evidence-heavy research, and two-on-two rounds where students build and defend a plan against every angle of attack. The format behind the open-source briefs below.

Public Forum Debate

Monthly topics drawn from live public controversy, judged for persuasion of an intelligent non-specialist — the format that teaches students to be understood, not just correct.

Political Gamification

An experimental curriculum where students role-play political avatars through debates and strategy games. Pre/post surveys and a self-reflection rubric measure anxiety, evidence usage, rule adherence, and whether students can explain what bias is and why people write evidence.

Debate Social Skills

Debate as a social-skills intervention, connected to my research adapting the ASCS-2 autism assessment for measuring social skills in a debate context.

Research instruments & coursework

  • ASCS2-DB8 — a debate-context adaptation of the ASCS-2 autism assessment for measuring social skills, prototyped in a three-day intensive with first-grade students.
  • Classroom case study — operationally defined a head-orientation target behavior for a grade-6 autistic student, interrogated social validity, collected baseline, applied a reinforcement-based EBP, and recorded interval data across four debates.
  • This website — teaches parents and general educators how to read psychometric assessments, and addresses common controversies and pseudoscientific claims in autism intervention. Built openly with AI: see How This Site Is Built.
