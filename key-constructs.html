15 Key Constructs

SPED 860 · Fall 2024 · Dr. Glennda McKeithan

Key constructs in the education of children and youth with autism spectrum disorder.

1. Structured Teaching
Creates a visual map of the learning environment that autistic learners can understand.
2. TEACCH Program
Developed at the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill to study Structured Teaching.
3. Culture of Autism
Defines ASD as an alternative, acceptable form of relating to the world rather than a disorder with a cure.
4. Behavioral Communication
Forms of behavior expressed by learners with ASD with intent to communicate meaning without formal language.
5. Functional Communication
Expressing oneself through language and avoiding negative behaviors.
6. Direct Communication
Intentional communication that conveys meaning effectively, accurately, and precisely.
7. Teaching Social Skills in ASD
Social skills can be taught in a variety of ways, including groups of learners with and without ASD. Groups should be structured and have measurable, observable outcomes.
8. Social Skills and Academic Competence
Academic competence is strongly linked to social inclusion because the two causally influence each other over time.
9. Sexuality Education for Individuals with ASD
Individuals on the spectrum may feel their sexual orientation and/or behavior differs from neurotypical peers. The Sexuality Information and Education Council of the United States offers free resources to support ASD learners in their sexual development.
10. Indirect Communication
Communication expressed through facial expressions, body language, or other non-verbal cues. Learners with ASD process this information differently and need support to learn what it means.
11. Social Skills as Learned Behaviors
Social skills are NOT innate; they are learned. Because ASD learners process social information differently, teachers can adapt lessons to teach these skills.
12. Communication Skills
Necessary for human relationships. Some learners with ASD communicate with speech and others cannot; many struggle with the rhythm and meaning of words and with indirect communication. Effective communicators can transfer information from one place to another.
13. Structured Teaching Pyramid
TEACCH developed the structured teaching pyramid to describe the necessary visual structure of a structured teaching classroom.
14. Social Disconnection
People with autism frequently desire friendship and social connection, yet may appear uninterested in the social world or not understand it.
15. Schedules of Reinforcement
The timing and frequency of reinforcement in response to a target behavior. It can be Fixed, Variable, or Continuous.
