Evidence Briefs

AFIRM briefs for the practices discussed on this site

The briefs below support the evidence-based practices mentioned across this site. All of them were prepared by the AFIRM team at the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill. AFIRM stands for Autism Focused Intervention Resources and Modules, and is an extension of the National Professional Development Center on Autism.

Each brief contains step-by-step instructions, checklists for implementation and data collection, and the evidence base supporting the practice. Interventionists in schools, clinics, or homes can use them to learn the practice or to check fidelity of implementation.

These briefs are thorough and contain what a trained individual needs to plan and execute these practices. They are not a replacement for professional development, and they do not contain enough context or examples for an absolute beginner.

Reinforcement

Following a target behavior with a consequence that increases the likelihood it happens again.

View on AFIRM

Visual Supports

Schedules, organizers, and structured-teaching maps that support comprehension and independence.

View on AFIRM

Video Modeling

Demonstrating a target skill on video for the learner to imitate.

View on AFIRM

Response Interruption & Redirection

Interrupting an interfering behavior and redirecting the learner to a more appropriate one.

View on AFIRM

Discrete Trial Training

Breaking a task into small, teachable chunks that a learner repeats until mastered.

View on AFIRM

AAC

Augmentative and alternative communication — giving non-speaking learners a means to communicate.

View on AFIRM
