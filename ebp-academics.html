EBPs for Improving Academic Outcomes

Practices with empirical support

These evidence-based practices have empirical support for improving academic, cognitive, and adaptive outcomes for learners with autism, as identified in the National Clearinghouse on Autism Evidence and Practice (NCAEP) review.

Task Analysis
Breaking a complex skill into smaller, sequential steps that are taught one at a time and then chained together.
Prompting
Verbal, gestural, model, or physical assistance that helps a learner acquire or perform a target skill, then is systematically faded.
Time Delay
Inserting a brief, controlled wait between an instruction and a prompt to transfer the skill to independence and fade reliance on prompts.
Visual Supports
Visual schedules, graphic organizers, scripts, and structured-teaching maps that support comprehension, transitions, and independent work.
Technology-Aided Instruction & Intervention (TAII)
Instruction in which technology (apps, devices, software) is the central feature supporting a learning goal.
Modeling & Video Modeling
Demonstrating a target skill — live or on video — for the learner to imitate.
Self-Management
Teaching learners to discriminate, record, and reinforce their own behavior, building toward independence and self-determination.
Reinforcement
Following a target behavior with a consequence that increases the likelihood the behavior occurs again.

Source: Steinbrenner, J. R., Hume, K., Odom, S. L., et al. (2020). Evidence-Based Practices for Children, Youth, and Young Adults with Autism. National Clearinghouse on Autism Evidence and Practice (NCAEP), UNC Frank Porter Graham Child Development Institute.
